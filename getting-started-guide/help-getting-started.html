Your first steps with OnionPress

OnionPress runs your website from your own Mac and publishes it as an onion service on Tor. This guide shows where to click to open your dashboard, write a post, see your site, and keep a backup.

1Find the OnionPress icon

Once OnionPress is running, a small purple onion sits in the menu bar at the top right of your screen, near the clock. Everything else in this guide starts from that icon.

Can’t see it?

If you have more than one screen, look at the menu bar on the screen you’re using. On MacBooks with a notch, a crowded menu bar can hide icons behind the notch, so quit an app or two you don’t need. If the onion isn’t there at all, open OnionPress from your Applications folder.

2The OnionPress menu

Click the onion to open the menu. You’ll only need a few of its items day to day.

  1. Status line. Shows your site’s name and onion address, like orbit@op2auss3….onion. While OnionPress is starting up it says Starting... instead.
  2. Open Local Site is the one to remember. It opens your site with you already signed in, so you can get to your dashboard (chapter 3).
  3. Copy Onion Address copies your site’s address so you can share it.
  4. Open in Brave Browser opens your site over Tor, the way visitors see it (chapter 5). If you use Tor Browser, this item says Open in Tor Browser.
  5. Backup... saves a copy of your whole site (chapter 6).

3Get into your dashboard

Your site runs on WordPress, and the dashboard is where you write and manage it. To get there, click the onion and choose Open Local Site. Your browser opens your site, already signed in.

  1. Posts lists everything you’ve written.
  2. + New starts a new post.
  3. Your site name (here, Orbit’s Universe) takes you to your site as visitors see it.

Seeing a login page instead?

If your browser shows a WordPress login page asking for a username and password, don’t try to guess them. Go back to the onion and choose Open Local Site again.

Why there’s no password to type

Open Local Site signs you in with a one-time link. Each link works once and expires after 2 minutes, so bookmarking it won’t work. Once you’re signed in, your browser usually stays signed in. You can still get signed out, for example after a restart, after clearing your browser data, or in a different browser. Whenever that happens, click Open Local Site again.

4Write and publish your first post

In the dashboard, click + New at the top. The editor opens.

  1. Click Add title and type a title.
  2. Click below the title and start writing. Press Return for a new paragraph. To add a picture or other content, type / and choose from the list.
  3. Click Publish, then click Publish again to confirm. Your post is now live.

To check how it looks, click your site name at the top left of the dashboard. Your newest post appears at the top of your home page.

  1. Your name and onion address. This is the address people use to reach your site.
  2. Howdy, orbit means you’re signed in. Visitors don’t see this dark bar.

5See your site the way visitors do

Open Local Site shows your site straight from your Mac. To see it over Tor, the way visitors reach it, click the onion and choose Open in Brave Browser (or Open in Tor Browser).

The first time, the page stays blank for a while. Tor is building a private route to your site through several computers around the world. 20 to 30 seconds is normal, and sometimes it takes longer.

  1. The .onion address in the address bar. Anyone with Tor can visit it.
  2. The Tor label means this window connects through Tor.
Sharing your site

Choose Copy Onion Address from the onion menu and paste it anywhere. Visitors need Tor Browser, or a Brave Private Window with Tor, to open .onion addresses.

6Keep a backup

A backup saves your whole site in one file: posts, pictures, settings, and the keys to your onion address. With it, you can restore your site on another Mac, at the same address. Make one after anything you’d hate to lose.

Click the onion and choose Backup....

  1. Username is filled in for you.
  2. Type your password. This is the one you chose during setup. It also locks the backup file, and you’ll need it to restore.
  3. Click Backup, choose where to save, and click Save. When it’s done, OnionPress tells you the file name and size.
Forgot your password?

Open your dashboard with Open Local Site (no password needed), then go to Users → Profile and click Set New Password. Save the new one in a password manager. Backups you made earlier still need the old password.

Backups go in the OnionPress/backups folder in your home folder, unless you choose somewhere else.

For extra safety, copy the backup somewhere other than your Mac, like an external drive.
